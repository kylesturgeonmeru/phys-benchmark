# US aesthetics demand, Q1 2019 to Q3 2026: market or company?

One-time read for judging whether ADCS's aesthetics weakness is the market or the company. Public sources only. Files: `aesthetics_demand.xlsx` (raw_by_company, index, commentary_log, events, sources, plus an extra price_vs_volume tab), `aesthetics_demand_index.png`.

## Short answers

**1. How deep was the 2024 to 2025 decline, by category?**
Shallow for toxins and moderate for fillers, on a US manufacturer sell-in basis.
- Neurotoxins: down 5.3% in 2025 versus 2024 (AbbVie, Evolus, and Galderma combined). The quarterly low was Q1 2025, 23% below the Q2 2024 peak, and the index was back near its 2024 level by Q4 2025.
- Fillers: down 6.0% in 2025 versus 2024, and about 13% below 2023. The filler index sits roughly 23% below its 2019 average (77 versus 100) while toxins sit at 184.
- Without Galderma the declines look worse (toxins down 8.7%, fillers down 13.1%), because AbbVie was the one shrinking. Galderma matters little for the toxin level (about 6 index points on average since 2024) and a lot for the filler read (about 7 points, and nearly double the 2025 decline).
- AbbVie alone: US Botox Cosmetic down 10.6%, US Juvederm down 17.9%. Evolus Jeuveau grew about 3%. Estimated Galderma US neuromodulators grew about 7%.

**2. Was it volume or price?**
Different answers by category, and a share shift in the middle.
- Toxins: mostly price and share, and mostly AbbVie-specific. AbbVie's filings blame unfavorable pricing from its consumer loyalty program changes and lower market share, with weaker consumer demand third. Galderma and Evolus grew over the same period, which is what a share shift looks like. I called Q1 and Q3 2025 price or promotions, Q2 2025 share shift.
- Fillers: mostly volume. AbbVie cites decreased consumer demand in nearly every quarter of 2025, and Galderma describes a soft filler market with heavy promotions and pricing pressure in the mid-face. Price is a real second factor, and the filings do not let me split it cleanly.
- 2024 was mostly timing and pricing: destocking in Q1, favorable pricing in Q2 and Q3.
- The call per quarter, with confidence and rationale, is in the price_vs_volume tab. It is judgment on the quoted commentary, not a regression.

**3. Is 2026 a real recovery or toxin only?**
Mostly toxin, and part of the headline is base effects.
- Year to date through Q3 2026, toxins are up 8.6% and fillers up 4.0% versus the same period of 2025.
- Q1 2026 was flattered: AbbVie cites lapping the loyalty-program pricing hit and the timing of customer stocking, and Galderma cites favorable US phasing that unwound in Q2. Excluding Q1, toxins are up 4.9% in Q2 and Q3, and fillers are down 0.9%.
- Against 2024 rather than 2025, 2026 year to date is flat for toxins (up 0.2%) and down 4.9% for fillers. That is a stabilization with a toxin lean, not a rebound. AbbVie's own filler disclosures still say decreased consumer demand through Q3 2026.

**4. What would ADCS's aesthetics revenue have done if it simply tracked the market?**
I do not have ADCS aesthetics revenue (the repo holds only a name-match file), so this is expressed as percent changes to apply to ADCS's own figures.
- Market-tracking, 2025 versus 2024: toxins down 5.3%, fillers down 6.0%. Weighting by the 2024 sell-in mix (74% toxin, 26% filler) gives about down 5.5%. Per $100 of 2024 ADCS aesthetics revenue, $94.50 in 2025.
- 2026 year to date versus 2025 year to date: toxins up 8.6%, fillers up 4.0%, blended about up 7%. Versus 2024 year to date, blended about down 1%.
- Reading it against ADCS: a drop much deeper than about 5 to 6% in 2025 is company-specific. A drop of that size is consistent with the market. Use ADCS's own toxin and filler mix, since a filler-heavy practice mix drifts toward the filler line.

## Caveats

- Manufacturer revenue is sell-in to practices, not patient treatments. Destocking, stocking, loyalty-program accounting, and phasing all move it without moving patient volume.
- Single-company reads are distorted by share shifts: AbbVie lost share, Galderma and Evolus gained. The combined series dampens this but does not remove it.
- US category splits are partly estimated. Galderma reports only group-level US versus International, so US neuromodulators and fillers are modeled (method in the sources tab: group US less 90% of Nemluvio, times the injectables share of the rest, split by global mix). That estimate swings with Galderma's quarterly phasing, so single quarters are noisy. Galderma is also absent before 2023, and the full index is chained from AbbVie plus Evolus until then. AbbVie Q2 2020 is an estimate, because AbbVie reported only 8 May onward.
- Merz and other private players (Revance and BeautyHealth were optional and not pulled) are excluded.

## Other things to know

- Evolus is treated as US-weighted. International was about 2% of revenue in 2022, 3% in 2023, and 5% in 2024. Later years were not parsed.
- The commentary log was extracted by sentence rules and keyword tags, with quotes trimmed to under 40 words. I spot-checked it, but the driver tags are heuristic. Some quarters, such as AbbVie 2020 and early 2021, have no explanatory text in the filings.
- Event dates marked "public record" in the events tab (rate hikes, GLP-1 approvals, Daxxify, COVID closure timing) were not re-verified in this session. Filing-sourced events are labeled as such.
- The AbbVie loyalty program change is dated to Q1 2025, when the 10-Q first cites it. The exact effective date is not stated in the filing.
